The Oasis of Qaseem: Desert Crown Of Mars

Honoring the Vision of Saudi Arabia upon Mars.

"By the noonday brightness,
by the night when it darkeneth,
thy Lord hath not forsaken thee,
neither hath He been displeased.
And surely the future shall be better for thee than the past,
and in the end shall thy Lord be bounteous to thee,

Submitted for your consideration: a legacy written not in sand, but in stars. The Oasis of Qaseem—where the ancient art of the desert chase has evolved into the Qaseem Sabaq, a ballet of anti-gravity craft racing across the rust. But this is more than a sport. It is the spirit of a people who mastered the most unforgiving dunes on Earth, now applied to taming a planet. What you are about to witness is not merely a race. It is a culture in motion, a testament that the same heart that finds the divine in the silence of the desert can also find it in the roar of a Martian engine.

THE SPORT OF A NEW HOMELAND

The Qaseem Sabaq is more than a race—it's the kinetic expression of a culture that refined survival in Earth's most brutal deserts. Here on Mars, that same spirit of endurance, precision, and honor has been translated into the most thrilling spectacle on the Red Planet.

Custom-built hovercraft, inspired by the elegant geometry of Islamic art and powered by repurposed atmospheric mining thrusters, skim centimeters above the rust. Pilots—many descended from Bedouin navigators—rely on instinct and augmented reality displays that overlay traditional star-path diagrams onto Martian topography.

The Sabaq is the public face of the Oasis of Qaseem, but it fuels the dome's true mission: the quiet, revolutionary work in water reclamation and atmospheric processing that sustains colonies across Mars, and the asteroid belt. The race is the heartbeat; the science is the breath.

This is what happens when a civilization looks at a wasteland and sees not a barrier, but a proving ground.

They said a line was the future. On Earth, they dreamed of The Line—a straight city cutting through the desert. But on Mars, we learned that survival bends. It adapts. It connects.

"A crescent bends — and in that curve, finds strength a straight line never knew."

Thus was born The Dome Crescent — a ribbon of linked domes arcing across Utopia Planitia. When viewed from orbit, as light catches the regolith shielding, it glows with the unmistakable curve of a half-moon suspended on the rust.

🐋 The Sultan's Gift: A Fair for All

Where Mars Comes to Play

The Sultan of the Crescent built the biggest dome on Mars — a wonder of engineering and water reclamation. Within it, he placed a gift for every citizen: a public fair unlike any other. The famous whales of Mars have found a home here. Children from every nation come to see them breach in the artificial sun. They are not the attraction — they are the soul of it.

The fair is open to all. A miner stands beside a prince in line for the Dust-Storm Simulator. The Souk of a Thousand Stars brings vendors from the Belt to Earth. And at the center of it all, the Crescent Coaster shoots outside the dome, giving riders a 10-second view of pure Martian landscape.
